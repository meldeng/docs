title: "Apple Pay"
description: "Present the system Apple Pay sheet inside your own app or site."
---

Your backend mints the order, the [Meld SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks) presents the sheet, and settlement arrives by webhook.

## Before you begin

1. [Create a customer](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc/meld-kyces-the-user#step-1-create-a-meld-customer) and complete [Unified KYC](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc). Required — almost every provider refuses an order for a customer Meld has not cleared
2. [Configure webhooks](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/configure-webhooks), before KYC so you receive status changes while it runs

Stripe and Coinbase are the exceptions to step 1. Meld runs no KYC for either:

- Stripe verifies the customer inside the flow its SDK presents, and needs only an email on the Meld customer. See [Stripe](#stripe).
- Coinbase needs a verified email and phone and the customer's acceptance of its agreements. It asks for identity only once the customer reaches its limits. See [Coinbase](#coinbase).

## Apple Developer setup

Whether you need any depends on how the provider presents the sheet.

| How the sheet is presented | Providers today | Setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natively, by your app | Mercuryo, Banxa | Merchant identifier, payment processing certificate, Apple Pay entitlement |
| Natively, by the provider's own SDK | Stripe | Your own merchant identifier, a payment processing certificate issued from Stripe's CSR, Apple Pay entitlement. See [Stripe setup](#stripe-setup) |
| By the provider, as merchant of record on a page it hosts | Coinbase | None |

You never branch on this in code — the SDK presents whichever surface the order carries. It only decides what you register with Apple up front, so do it once, before your first order with the provider concerned.

**You will need:**

- An Apple Developer account with admin rights on your team
- The payment processing CSR, which your Meld representative provides. For Stripe, your own Stripe Dashboard provides it
- Xcode, to add the capability and regenerate provisioning profiles

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a merchant identifier">
    A merchant identifier names your app to Apple Pay. It lives under your Apple Developer account, not Meld's.

    1. Go to [Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles](https://developer.apple.com/account/resources/identifiers/list)
    2. Click **Identifiers** in the sidebar, then **+**
    3. Select **Merchant IDs**, then **Continue**
    4. Enter a description and an identifier — it must start with `merchant.` (e.g. `merchant.com.yourcompany.yourapp`)
    5. Click **Continue**, review, then **Register**

    If your app already has a merchant identifier, reuse it, unless another processor's certificate is on it. See [Supporting more than one provider](#supporting-more-than-one-provider).

    Give your Meld representative the identifier for each environment you use. Meld configures it on your account, and your orders carry it to the SDK. Send it well before your first order in that environment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a payment processing certificate">
    This certificate is what the Apple Pay token is encrypted to. It must be created from a CSR **Meld provides** — never generate your own for this step, because the processor holds the matching private key. For Stripe, the CSR comes from your Stripe account instead — see [Stripe setup](#stripe-setup).

    Once you have the `.certSigningRequest` file from your Meld representative:

    1. Go to [Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles](https://developer.apple.com/account/resources/identifiers/list)
    2. Click **Identifiers**, filter by **Merchant IDs**, and select the one from Step 1
    3. Under **Apple Pay Payment Processing Certificate**, click **Create Certificate**
    4. Upload the `.certSigningRequest` file Meld provided
    5. Click **Continue**, then **Download** to save the `.cer` file
    6. Send the `.cer` file back to your Meld representative

    Meld passes it to the processor. Wait for confirmation before going further — payments fail until the processor holds it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure Xcode">
    1. Open your project in Xcode and select your app target
    2. Go to the **Signing & Capabilities** tab
    3. Click **+ Capability** and select **Apple Pay**
    4. Under the Apple Pay capability, click **+** and add the merchant identifier from Step 1
    5. Regenerate provisioning profiles

    On React Native, the [Expo config plugin](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/react-native) does this for you — pass the same merchant identifier.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Certificate expiry.** Apple payment processing certificates expire every 25 months. When yours approaches expiry, repeat Step 2 — Meld will provide a fresh CSR. An expired certificate fails every payment.
</Note>

### Supporting more than one provider

Use one merchant identifier per processor. Apple encrypts a merchant identifier's tokens to a single payment processing certificate, so two processors cannot share one. If you offer Apple Pay through two providers, run the setup once for each, with its own identifier.

Your app's Apple Pay entitlement must hold every identifier: add each in Step 3, or list each in the Expo plugin's `merchantIds`. The sheet does not open for an identifier the entitlement lacks.

### Stripe setup

Stripe's SDK presents the sheet under **your** merchant identifier, and Stripe decrypts the token, so the CSR comes from Stripe, not Meld:

- In your Stripe Dashboard, open the [iOS certificate settings](https://dashboard.stripe.com/settings/ios_certificates), add an application and download the CSR. Upload it in Step 2.
- Upload the `.cer` file Apple returns to Stripe, not to Meld. Renew the same way when the certificate nears expiry.

Do this once for each Stripe environment you use: your Stripe sandbox for Meld's sandbox, and your live Stripe account for production. Each issues its own CSR, so each needs its own merchant identifier. Download the sandbox CSR inside your Stripe sandbox, and upload the `.cer` file back to that sandbox. Certificates you add to your live account do not carry over to it.

Give your Meld representative the merchant identifier for each environment. Until Meld has configured it, Stripe Apple Pay orders in that environment fail with `400 PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Stripe card orders need no merchant identifier.

### Registering your domain — web only

On the web there is no native sheet: the button renders on **your** origin, so that origin has to be registered with the provider before Apple will let it present one.

Registration is not self-serve. **Contact your Meld representative with the domains you need registered** — Meld brokers each one with the provider, so allow for a round trip per domain, and do it before you plan to go live. Every domain you serve the button from needs its own registration, staging included.

Native-only integrations skip this entirely.

## 1. Quote, then check eligibility

Every order starts here, not just the first one.

[Get a quote](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/quotes) — it prices the order, names the providers that can take it, and carries the `destinationNetworkCode` the order needs. Then [check eligibility](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/eligibility) for the provider you picked: agreements to show, a [verified email or phone](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/verification) to collect, KYC the provider still wants. Collect whatever is outstanding before you mint the order, or the rejection lands after the customer thinks they are done.

## 2. Create the order

**Endpoint:** [`POST /crypto/order/headless/onramp`](/api-reference/crypto/headless/crypto-order-headless-onramp-create)

**Headers:** `Meld-Version: 2026-05-01`, `X-Idempotency-Key: <uuid>`

```json
{
  "customerId": "WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee",
  "externalOrderId": "order-3ba9f1",
  "serviceProvider": "MERCURYO",
  "paymentMethodType": "APPLE_PAY",
  "countryCode": "FR",
  "sourceAmount": "100",
  "sourceCurrencyCode": "EUR",
  "destinationCurrencyCode": "USDC",
  "destinationWalletAddress": "0x51FB80013111111111111112121111111",
  "destinationNetworkCode": "ETH",
  "clientIpAddress": "203.0.113.24"
}
```

Send the customer's public IP as `clientIpAddress`. The SDK takes the IP and the destination wallet from the order, so you do not pass them to it again.

Response, for this Mercuryo order. `...` marks values elided here:

```json
{
  "id": "WoRdErabcexample0000002",
  "customerId": "WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee",
  "externalOrderId": "order-3ba9f1",
  "paymentMethodType": "APPLE_PAY",
  "responseKind": "SESSION",
  "paymentMethodResponseDetails": {
    "presentation": "NATIVE_TOKEN",
    "sessionToken": "...",
    "merchantTransactionId": "...",
    "merchantIdentifier": "merchant.com.yourcompany.app"
  },
  "payload": { "...": "echo of your request" },
  "paymentActions": { "...": "read by the SDK" },
  "headlessPresentation": {
    "surface": "NATIVE_TOKEN",
    "protocol": "MELD_WALLET_TOKEN",
    "version": 1
  }
}
```

Return it to your client verbatim. `paymentMethodResponseDetails`, `paymentActions` and `headlessPresentation` carry what the SDK needs. Your app does not have to read those fields itself, but it must not drop them.

Its shape depends on the provider. A Stripe order carries a bootstrap for Stripe's SDK, and a Coinbase order carries a link to Coinbase's checkout instead of a session token. See [Stripe](#create-a-stripe-order) and [Coinbase](#create-a-coinbase-order).

## 3. Present the sheet

Check the device can pay before you offer the button, then hand the order to the [SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks).

<CodeGroup>

```tsx React Native
import { Meld, MeldWidget } from '@meldcrypto/react-native-sdk';

if (await Meld.canPresentApplePay()) {
  // show the Apple Pay button
}

<MeldWidget
  order={order}
  applePay={{
    amount: '100.00',            // must match the order
    currencyCode: 'EUR',
    email: customerEmail,        // used only when the sheet returns none
    summaryItemLabel: 'Acme — Buy USDC',
  }}
  onPaymentSubmitted={onPaymentSubmitted}
  onCancel={onCancel}
  onError={onError}
/>
```

```swift iOS
import MeldSDK

guard Meld.canPresentApplePay() else {
    // No card in Wallet, or Apple Pay is restricted. Fall back to card.
    return
}

let handle = try Meld.mount(order, applePay: MeldApplePayRequest(
    amount: 100.00,
    currencyCode: "EUR",                  // must match the order
    email: customerEmail,                 // used only when the sheet returns none
    summaryItemLabel: "Acme — Buy USDC"
), handlers: MeldEventHandlers(
    onReady:            { _ in },
    onPaymentSubmitted: { _ in showProcessing() },
    onStatusChange:     { status in track(status) },
    onCancel:           { _ in backToAmount() },
    onError:            { error in showError(error.message) }
))
```

```js Web
import Meld from '@meldcrypto/sdk';

// The provider's component renders its own Apple Pay button and opens the sheet from
// that tap — Apple requires the gesture inside the frame holding the merchant session.
// Do not render a button of your own in front of it.
Meld.mount(order, document.getElementById('checkout'), {
  onReady:            ()  => hideSpinner(),
  onPaymentSubmitted: ()  => showProcessing(),
  onCancel:           ()  => backToAmount(),
  onError:            (e) => showError(e.message),
});
```

</CodeGroup>

On React Native and iOS, pass the Apple Pay inputs for every Apple Pay order, so your app never branches on the provider. They carry what the sheet shows and the order does not. A Coinbase order ignores them, and on a Stripe order they must match the order's amount, in USD. On the web there is nothing extra to pass: the order carries it, and the provider's component draws the button.

A native sheet draws nothing into your view, because iOS puts it over your screen. The React Native component still has to stay mounted for as long as the sheet is up. A zero-size component, mounted from the customer's tap, is enough.

`onCancel` fires when the user dismisses the sheet. Treat it as a change of mind and return them to the amount screen.

<Note>
  Not every provider's Apple Pay works on every platform. Stripe and Coinbase run only in an iOS app. Call `Meld.capabilities(order)` (`Meld.capabilities(for: order)` on iOS) before you offer the button, and fall back when its `surface` is `unsupported`. An order the SDK cannot present reports that rather than failing at mount.
</Note>

## 4. Settlement

`onPaymentSubmitted` means the user authorised the sheet. Authorisation is not settlement.

Unmount when it fires and move the user to your own processing screen. Credit them when Meld's [`TRANSACTION_CRYPTO_COMPLETE` webhook](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/configure-webhooks) reaches your backend — no SDK event tells you the money moved.

## Stripe

Stripe runs the purchase in its own SDK — Link sign-in, identity checks, the Apple Pay sheet and checkout — and the Meld SDK drives it. Your backend's part does not change: quote, create the order, return it verbatim. Stripe card orders run the same flow; [Cards](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/cards#stripe) covers the one difference.

### Scope

| | Stripe headless |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | US only, excluding New York. Stripe decides which states it serves, and Meld does not check the state up front. Stripe refuses an unserved customer inside the flow: before payment, the SDK reports `PRESENTATION_FAILED`; at payment, see [When Stripe refuses the payment](#when-stripe-refuses-the-payment) |
| Currency | USD in. The headless quote lists the tokens and networks |
| Payment methods | `APPLE_PAY` and `CREDIT_DEBIT_CARD` |
| Clients | iOS, directly or through the [React Native SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/react-native#stripe-orders). Not the web SDK, not Android |
| KYC | None by Meld. The headless quote reports `kycMode: PROVIDER_MANAGED`, and Stripe verifies the customer inside the flow |
| Credentials | Your own Stripe account, onboarded by Stripe for its crypto onramp and set up on Meld for headless orders. See [Before you begin](#before-you-begin-with-stripe) |
| Environments | Sandbox and production. In sandbox, use your Stripe sandbox and its test keys — see [Test Stripe](/docs/stablecoins/sandbox-guide/headless-testing#test-stripe) |

### Before you begin with Stripe

- **Stripe onboarding.** Stripe onboards your account for its embedded crypto onramp, which is in private preview. Onboarding enables the onramp and Link, provisions a Link OAuth client ID and client secret, gives you the API version for the onramp, and registers your app with Stripe as a trusted application. No Stripe call succeeds before it is complete, in a Stripe sandbox too. Meld sends each order's fee-inclusive total as `source_total_amount`, so confirm with Stripe that your account accepts it.
- **Your Stripe integration on Meld.** Add your Stripe publishable key, secret key and webhook signing secret to your Meld account, as in [provider setup](/docs/stablecoins/for-all-products/service-provider-setup). Match the integration's environment and keys to the Meld environment: on your sandbox account, set the environment to Sandbox and use test keys (`pk_test_`, `sk_test_`); in production, set it to Production and use live keys. The integration's environment decides the order's `sdkEnvironment`, so a sandbox account whose integration is set to Production creates orders the SDK refuses.
- **Headless fields.** On the same integration in the Meld dashboard, also enter the Link OAuth client ID and client secret that Stripe provisioned, and the headless API version Stripe gave you: a dated version ending in `;crypto_onramp_beta=v2`. Use the values from the same Stripe environment as your keys. The headless API version is not the widget's version on the provider setup page.
- **Stripe webhooks to Meld.** Meld settles Stripe orders only from Stripe's webhooks. In Stripe, add a webhook endpoint at the webhook URL the Meld dashboard shows for your Stripe integration, subscribed to `crypto.onramp_session.updated`, and save its signing secret as the integration's webhook secret.
- **An email on the customer.** Meld starts Link sign-in with the email on the Meld customer record. Without one, the order is refused with `403 VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`; [eligibility](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/eligibility) reports it before you create the order.
- **[Stripe setup](#stripe-setup)**, for Apple Pay.
- Unified KYC is optional. If the customer has passed it, the SDK fills Stripe's KYC from it — see [Prefill](#prefill).

### Create a Stripe order

Send the [step 2](#2-create-the-order) request with `serviceProvider: STRIPE`, `countryCode: US`, `sourceCurrencyCode: USD` and the quote's `destinationNetworkCode`. Both `destinationNetworkCode` and the `X-Idempotency-Key` header are required.

Creating the order charges nothing. Meld starts Link sign-in for the customer's email and returns a bootstrap for Stripe's SDK. This sandbox example shows every field the SDK needs; `...` marks values elided here:

```json
{
  "id": "WoRdErabcexample0000003",
  "customerId": "WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee",
  "externalOrderId": "order-5c71d2",
  "paymentMethodType": "APPLE_PAY",
  "responseKind": "SDK_BOOTSTRAP",
  "paymentMethodResponseDetails": {
    "sdkBootstrapType": "STRIPE_CRYPTO_ONRAMP",
    "providerIntentId": "lai_...",
    "continuationToken": "...",
    "sdkFlow": "AUTHORIZE",
    "sdkEnvironment": "SANDBOX",
    "expiresAtEpochSeconds": 1791300000,
    "clientConfiguration": {
      "publicKey": "pk_test_...",
      "walletNetwork": "ethereum",
      "merchantIdentifier": "merchant.com.yourcompany.app.stripe.sandbox"
    }
  },
  "payload": { "...": "echo of your request" },
  "paymentActions": { "...": "read by the SDK" },
  "headlessPresentation": {
    "surface": "NATIVE_SDK",
    "protocol": "STRIPE_CRYPTO_ONRAMP",
    "version": 1
  }
}
```

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `continuationToken` | A short-lived credential for this order. Never log it |
| `expiresAtEpochSeconds` | When the bootstrap stops working. Past it, create a new order |
| `sdkEnvironment` | `SANDBOX` or `PRODUCTION`: your Stripe integration's environment setting. It must match the environment you configure the SDK for |
| `clientConfiguration.publicKey` | Your Stripe publishable key: `pk_test_` in sandbox, `pk_live_` in production |
| `clientConfiguration.merchantIdentifier` | Apple Pay only. Your Stripe merchant identifier for this environment — see [Stripe setup](#stripe-setup) |

Return the response verbatim, as with every order. The SDK refuses a Stripe order that lacks any field it needs, so a backend that forwards selected fields breaks it.

The SDK then continues the order from the device, calling Meld with the `continuationToken`. You never make those calls yourself: their outcomes reach you as [SDK callbacks](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/ios#stripe-orders), and settlement works as in [step 4](#4-settlement).

The SDK makes those calls to the Meld environment it is configured for, so configure it for the one you created the order in: `sandbox` for orders created on `https://api-sb.meld.io`. It also checks the order's `sdkEnvironment` and key against that setting. On a mismatch, `Meld.capabilities` reports `unsupported` and mounting throws. A sandbox order from an integration set to Production fails this way.

### The Link email is the customer's email

Meld starts Link sign-in with the email on the Meld customer record, and the SDK [prefills](#prefill) that email, so the customer normally doesn't type one. As a result:

- Link sends its code to the phone on the Link account that email belongs to, not to a number your app collected
- If the email has no Link account, the SDK registers one with it

### Prefill

The SDK fills Stripe's screens from the Meld customer, so it asks the customer for as little as it can:

| Field | Sent when | The SDK uses it to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `email` | The customer's email is a valid address | Sign in to Link |
| `phone` | The phone is verified with Meld and in E.164 form | Register a new Link account without a form |
| `fullName` | The customer has a first and last name | Name that new Link account |
| `identity` | The customer passed [Unified KYC](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc): first and last name, date of birth, US residence address | Fill Stripe's first KYC request, so the customer types only what it lacks |

Prefill depends on the email. If the customer's email is not a valid address, Meld sends no prefill at all: the SDK asks for the email on its own **Sign in to Link** form, then asks for everything else.

The SSN is never prefilled. The customer types it into the SDK, which hands it straight to Stripe, so it reaches neither Meld nor your servers. If Stripe rejects a prefilled submission, the SDK shows the full form.

### When Stripe refuses the payment

Stripe can refuse to create the payment session, or fail the session afterwards, for example for an unsupported card. Meld also refuses a session from a device whose IP address is not public, before calling Stripe. In each case nothing was charged and nothing will settle. The SDK reports `onError` `PAYMENT_REJECTED`: offer another payment method or provider, on a new order.

Some refusals report `PAYMENT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` instead, because the device had already started the payment when they arrived:

- An amount over the customer's Stripe limit
- An identity check Stripe asks for when the session is created
- A refusal that answers the SDK's own retry of the request

No payment session exists in these cases, but the device cannot tell. Handle them like any `PAYMENT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`: track the order through your backend and never pay it again.

### Errors on order creation

Errors use the [standard envelope](/docs/meld-api/error-responses). On a Stripe order, these codes mean:

| HTTP | `code` | Cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `403` | `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` | The Meld customer has no email; `errors` names `email: MISSING` | Add one with `PATCH /accounts/customers/{customerId}`, then retry |
| `400` | `INVALID_CRYPTO_CURRENCY` | `destinationNetworkCode` is missing | Send the quote's `destinationNetworkCode`, with a new `X-Idempotency-Key` |
| `400` | `PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SUPPORTED` | No Stripe headless route for this country, currency pair, network and method. Or your Stripe integration cannot serve the order: it is not your account's own, its keys do not match its environment (a `pk_live_` key on an integration set to Sandbox, or the reverse), or, for Apple Pay, Meld has no merchant identifier for you in this environment | Re-quote with `integrationMode=HEADLESS` and carry its values into the order. Otherwise check your Stripe integration and, for Apple Pay, [Stripe setup](#stripe-setup) |
| `400` | `HEADLESS_NOT_SUPPORTED` | Meld's own checks refused the order before any call to Stripe: the order does not match a Stripe headless route, or Stripe headless is not available to your account | Re-quote with `integrationMode=HEADLESS` and send its values unchanged. If it persists, ask your Meld representative |
| `400` | `ACCOUNT_BAD_CONFIGURATION` | Your Stripe integration is not set up for headless orders: the Link OAuth client ID or secret, or the headless API version, is missing or malformed | Add the missing value to your Stripe integration in the Meld dashboard. See [headless fields](#before-you-begin-with-stripe) |
| `422` | `STRIPE_HEADLESS_PROVIDER_REJECTED` | Stripe refused to start Link sign-in, or you retried an order whose bootstrap had already failed or expired. Stripe's reason can be this customer, such as a Link connection they revoked, or your whole Stripe account: onboarding not complete, Link sign-in not enabled, or an invalid secret key | Nothing was charged. Create a new order. If every order fails this way, a new order will not help: check your Stripe onboarding and keys with Stripe |
| `409` | `HEADLESS_PAYMENT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` | Meld could not confirm with Stripe whether sign-in started | Nothing was charged. Create a new order rather than retrying in a loop |
| `425` | `HEADLESS_PAYMENT_IN_PROGRESS` | The same request is still being processed | Retry shortly with the same `X-Idempotency-Key` |

## Coinbase

Coinbase takes the payment on its own checkout page, as merchant of record, and the Meld SDK opens Coinbase's Apple Pay sheet from it. The customer sees only the sheet. Your backend's part is the same as for any provider — quote, create the order, return it verbatim — plus collecting the verified contacts and agreements Coinbase requires.

### Coinbase scope

| | Coinbase headless |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | US only, with a US mobile number |
| Currency | USD in. The headless quote lists the tokens and networks |
| Payment methods | `APPLE_PAY` only. Coinbase has no headless card path, and any other method is refused with `400 PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SUPPORTED` |
| Clients | iOS 16 or later, through the [iOS SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/ios#coinbase-orders) or the [React Native SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/react-native#coinbase-orders) on iOS. Not the web SDK, not Android |
| Apple setup | None. No merchant identifier, certificate or domain registration |
| KYC | None by Meld up front. The headless quote reports `kycMode: PROVIDER_MANAGED`. Coinbase asks for identity only to raise a customer's limit — see [Limits](#coinbase-limits) |
| Credentials | Coinbase set up on your Meld account. Contact your Meld representative |
| Environments | Production. In sandbox you can test everything up to presenting the order — see [Test Coinbase in sandbox](#test-coinbase-in-sandbox) |

### Before you begin with Coinbase

Coinbase takes an order only for a customer with a verified email, a verified US mobile number and a recorded acceptance of its agreements. Meld checks all three before it places the order. Collect them first, while the customer is still in your flow.

[Eligibility](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/eligibility) reports what is outstanding. Pass the customer and the full order intent, and Meld runs the same checks the order will:

```
GET /crypto/onramp/COINBASEPAY/requirements
  ?customerId=WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee
  &paymentMethodType=APPLE_PAY
  &sourceCurrencyCode=USD
  &sourceAmount=50
  &destinationCurrencyCode=USDC
  &destinationNetworkCode=ETH
  &countryCode=US
```

The email and phone fields read as on [Eligibility](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/eligibility#contact-verification). These are the parts specific to Coinbase:

```json
{
  "legalAgreements": [
    { "type": "GUEST_CHECKOUT_TERMS_OF_SERVICE", "url": "https://www.coinbase.com/legal/guest-checkout/us", "region": "US", "code": "GUEST_CHECKOUT_TERMS_OF_SERVICE:US" },
    { "type": "TERMS_OF_SERVICE", "url": "https://www.coinbase.com/legal/user_agreement", "code": "TERMS_OF_SERVICE" },
    { "type": "PRIVACY_POLICY", "url": "https://www.coinbase.com/legal/privacy", "code": "PRIVACY_POLICY" }
  ],
  "verificationRequirements": {
    "verifyWith": { "endpoint": "/accounts/customers/{customerId}/verifications", "serviceProvider": "COINBASEPAY" },
    "agreement": { "required": true, "maxAgeDays": 60, "codes": ["GUEST_CHECKOUT_TERMS_OF_SERVICE:US", "TERMS_OF_SERVICE", "PRIVACY_POLICY"] }
  },
  "customerStatus": {
    "agreement": { "satisfied": false, "reason": "MISSING", "unmetCodes": ["GUEST_CHECKOUT_TERMS_OF_SERVICE:US", "TERMS_OF_SERVICE", "PRIVACY_POLICY"] }
  }
}
```

Read the agreement codes from this response rather than hardcoding them.

#### Verify the email and phone

The customer record needs an email. Verify the email and the phone with [Meld's one-time code](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/verification), and send `serviceProvider: "COINBASEPAY"` on each trigger:

```json
{
  "channel": "PHONE",
  "target": "+14155550123",
  "serviceProvider": "COINBASEPAY"
}
```

- Send `serviceProvider` on both triggers. An email code sent without it does not count for Coinbase, and the order is still refused with `email: MISSING`.
- The phone must be a US mobile number, in international form.
- Coinbase accepts a verification for 60 days. After that, eligibility reports the channel as unsatisfied, and the customer verifies again.
- Coinbase receives the email and phone exactly as the customer verified them, not the values on the customer record.

#### Record the agreements

Show each document in `legalAgreements` in your own UI, and gate your flow on the customer accepting. Then record the acceptance in one of two ways:

1. **One receipt per code.** Call `POST /accounts/terms-of-service/acceptances` once for each code in `agreement.codes`:

   ```json
   {
     "externalCustomerId": "your-internal-user-id-123",
     "serviceProvider": "COINBASEPAY",
     "code": "GUEST_CHECKOUT_TERMS_OF_SERVICE:US",
     "acceptedAt": "2026-10-07T10:24:11Z",
     "ipAddress": "203.0.113.24"
   }
   ```

   `ipAddress` is the IP the customer accepted from, and it is required. Receipts are keyed by your `externalCustomerId`, so use the one on the Meld customer you order for. A customer without an `externalCustomerId` cannot hold receipts, so use the second way for that customer.

2. **An acceptance time on the order.** Send `verification.agreementAcceptedAt` on the create request: the instant the customer accepted the documents you showed, in ISO-8601. Keep your own record of which customer accepted which document, and when.

Meld reads receipts first, and uses `agreementAcceptedAt` when receipts do not cover every code. An acceptance counts for 60 days. Capture `agreementAcceptedAt` per order: a constant or a cached value stops counting once it is older than that. A value Meld cannot parse, or one in the future, is refused with `400` even when receipts cover the order.

#### Coinbase limits

The per-order minimum and maximum are on the [routes API](/docs/stablecoins/white-label-api-integration/whitelabel-api-guide#supported-routes), in `paymentMethods[].limits`.

Coinbase also limits each customer: a weekly spending cap, and a lifetime cap on the number of transactions. Meld checks the lifetime cap before it places the order. When the customer has reached it, eligibility reports a `PROVIDER_EXTRA_KYC` requirement with the `limitUpgrade` process, and the order is refused with `422 CUSTOMER_NOT_PAYMENT_READY`:

| `errors` | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| `limitUpgrade: REQUIRED` | Run the `limitUpgrade` process, then resend the same request |
| `limitUpgrade: PENDING` | Coinbase is reviewing the customer's upgrade. Wait, then re-check eligibility |
| `limitUpgrade: BLOCKED` | Coinbase will not raise the limit. Offer another provider |

To run the process, call `POST /crypto/onramp/COINBASEPAY/processes/limitUpgrade` with the customer and the order intent. Call it with `operation: READ` first, for its state and the input sources Coinbase offers. Then call it with `operation: START` and one of those sources. Never retry `START` automatically.

Coinbase applies the weekly cap itself, when it receives the order, and refuses an order over it. Meld cannot check it in advance.

### Create a Coinbase order

Send `walletAddress` on the [headless quote](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/quotes), so that Coinbase prices the same checkout the order uses. Then send the [step 2](#2-create-the-order) request with `serviceProvider: COINBASEPAY`, `paymentMethodType: APPLE_PAY`, `countryCode: US` and `sourceCurrencyCode: USD`:

- `clientIpAddress` is the customer's device IP, which Coinbase receives with the order. Without it, Meld uses the IP of the caller, which is your server.
- `externalOrderId` must be unique per order. Coinbase sees it as your order reference.
- Add `"verification": { "agreementAcceptedAt": "2026-10-07T10:24:11Z" }` only when you record acceptance that way. Leave it out when receipts cover every code.

Response:

```json
{
  "id": "WoRdErabcexample0000004",
  "customerId": "WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee",
  "externalOrderId": "order-7d40e1",
  "paymentMethodType": "APPLE_PAY",
  "responseKind": "SESSION",
  "paymentMethodResponseDetails": {
    "paymentLinkUrl": "https://pay.coinbase.com/...",
    "presentation": "PROVIDER_HOSTED"
  },
  "payload": { "...": "echo of your request" },
  "headlessPresentation": {
    "surface": "PROVIDER_HOSTED",
    "protocol": "COINBASE_APPLE_PAY",
    "version": 1
  }
}
```

A Coinbase order carries no `sessionToken` and no `merchantIdentifier`: `paymentLinkUrl` is Coinbase's checkout for this order. Return the response verbatim and let the SDK open it. Do not open, change or reuse the link yourself. It works once, and a used or expired link ends in `ORDER_STATE_CHANGED`.

### Present a Coinbase order

Mount the order like any other Apple Pay order, from the customer's tap on your Apple Pay button. Nothing about the call is specific to Coinbase:

- `into:` is optional, and any `applePay` inputs are ignored.
- The SDK opens Coinbase's Apple Pay sheet, and the customer sees only the sheet. `onReady` fires as it opens.
- `onPaymentSubmitted` means Coinbase accepted the payment. You can unmount then: the SDK lets Coinbase finish confirming on its own.
- `onCancel` means the customer dismissed the sheet before paying.

Errors use the SDK's [normalized codes](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/ios#error-codes). On a Coinbase order:

- `ORDER_STATE_CHANGED` means the link was used or has expired, and `PRESENTATION_FAILED` means the flow stopped before any payment, so no payment exists. Create a new order.
- `APPLE_PAY_UNAVAILABLE` means the device cannot make Apple Pay payments, and `PAYMENT_REJECTED` means Coinbase declined the payment. Offer another method or provider, on a new order.
- `PAYMENT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` means the outcome is unclear. Track the order through your backend and never pay it again.

`detail` carries Coinbase's own event and code, for your logs. The full callback table is under [iOS → Coinbase orders](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks/ios#coinbase-orders).

### Errors on Coinbase order creation

Errors use the [standard envelope](/docs/meld-api/error-responses). Every refusal except the last arrives before Meld places the order with Coinbase, so nothing is spent.

| HTTP | `code` | Cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `400` | `PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SUPPORTED` | The order is not `APPLE_PAY`, or Coinbase has no headless route for its country, currency pair and network | Coinbase takes Apple Pay only. Offer another provider for cards, or re-quote with `integrationMode=HEADLESS` |
| `403` | `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` | `errors` lists each unmet item: `email`, `phone` or `agreement`, each `MISSING` or `STALE` | Verify or record what is listed, then resend |
| `403` | `LEGAL_ACCEPTANCE_REQUIRED` | Only the agreement is unmet | Record the acceptance, or send a fresh `agreementAcceptedAt`, then resend |
| `422` | `CUSTOMER_NOT_PAYMENT_READY` | The customer reached Coinbase's lifetime limit; `errors` names `limitUpgrade: REQUIRED`, `PENDING` or `BLOCKED` | See [Coinbase limits](#coinbase-limits) |
| `400` | `TRANSACTION_REQUEST_VALIDATION_ERROR` | `agreementAcceptedAt` cannot be parsed, or is in the future | Send the instant the customer accepted, in ISO-8601 |
| `500` | `CUSTOMER_VERIFICATION_UNAVAILABLE` | Meld could not check the customer's verification | Retry the same request later |
| `422` | `HEADLESS_PROVIDER_REJECTED` | Coinbase refused the order after Meld placed it, so the order is spent. `serviceProviderDetails` carries Coinbase's `errorType` | Create a new order, with a new `X-Idempotency-Key` and a new `externalOrderId`. If `errors` lists `email: REJECTED` or `phone: REJECTED`, verify that channel again first |

### Lifecycle and webhooks

Coinbase reports a payment to Meld only when Meld asks, so Meld checks each order with Coinbase, starting about a minute after you create it. Expect a minute or more between `onPaymentSubmitted` and the first webhook.

Meld creates the transaction only once Coinbase reports the payment taken, or the order failed. Then the webhooks follow:

| Webhook | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `TRANSACTION_CRYPTO_PENDING` | Coinbase took the payment, and the crypto is on its way |
| `TRANSACTION_CRYPTO_COMPLETE` | The crypto was delivered. Credit the customer here |
| `TRANSACTION_CRYPTO_FAILED` | The order failed |

If the customer never pays, there is no transaction and no webhook. Meld stops checking the order after about 24 hours.

### Test Coinbase in sandbox

In sandbox, test your backend's part of a Coinbase order against `https://api-sb.meld.io`: the quote, eligibility, verification, agreements and order creation.

You need:

- Coinbase and headless onramps enabled on your sandbox account. Contact your Meld representative
- A real US mobile number and an email inbox you can read. Sandbox sends real one-time codes, so test numbers and test addresses do not work
- A destination wallet address that is valid on the network you quote

Then:

1. Quote US and USD with `integrationMode=HEADLESS` and your `walletAddress`, and pick the Coinbase quote.
2. Create the order with `paymentMethodType: APPLE_PAY` before the customer has verified anything. Expect `403 VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`, with `errors` listing what is outstanding, and check that your backend handles it.
3. [Check eligibility](#before-you-begin-with-coinbase). Verify the email and phone with `serviceProvider: "COINBASEPAY"`, and record the three agreements. Check eligibility again: email, phone and agreement are all `satisfied`.
4. Create the order again. Expect `201` with `responseKind: SESSION` and a `paymentLinkUrl`.

The iOS SDK 0.9.1 and the React Native SDK 0.8.0 cannot present a sandbox Coinbase order: mounting one ends in `onError` `PRESENTATION_FAILED`, and nothing is charged. Test the Apple Pay sheet, settlement and the transaction webhooks in production, with a small order.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Sheet does not appear | No card in Wallet, or the build's Apple Pay entitlement is missing or lacks the merchant identifier the order carries |
| Sheet appears, payment always declines | The processing certificate never got back to the processor, or the wrong key was used |
| Payment fails from the iOS Simulator | The Simulator cannot produce a usable Apple Pay token. Test on a physical device — see [sandbox testing](/docs/stablecoins/sandbox-guide/headless-testing) |
| `403` `WHITELABEL_NOT_ENABLED` | Headless onramps are not enabled on your account, in this environment. Contact your Meld representative |
| `403` `SERVICE_PROVIDER_NOT_ENABLED` | Your account has no credentials for that provider. See [provider credentials](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration#you-bring-your-own-provider-credentials) |
| `403` `KYC_NOT_COMPLETED` | Customer not approved with that provider. Re-check requirements before retrying |
| `400` `PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_SUPPORTED` | No headless Apple Pay route for this provider, country, currency pair and network. Re-quote with `integrationMode=HEADLESS`, or fall back to card. For Stripe, see [its errors](#errors-on-order-creation) |
| `404` naming a service-provider customer | Usually a missing `email` on the Meld customer, occasionally missing KYC. Stripe and Coinbase report a missing email as `403` `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` instead |
| `400` / `409` on `externalOrderId` | Reused value. Generate a fresh one per attempt |
| `INVALID_REDIRECT_URL` | You sent `redirectUrl`. Headless surfaces render in place, so the field is rejected |
| Surface never becomes ready | Content Security Policy on web, or a provider host not reachable from the device |
| User paid, no crypto yet | Normal while settlement runs. Wait for the webhook before showing a failure |

Errors specific to Stripe and Coinbase are under [Stripe](#errors-on-order-creation) and [Coinbase](#errors-on-coinbase-order-creation).

## Test in sandbox

Once your integration is complete, test it in Meld's sandbox, `https://api-sb.meld.io`, with the SDK configured for `sandbox`. [Headless testing](/docs/stablecoins/sandbox-guide/headless-testing) covers cards, Apple Pay and Stripe, and [Test Coinbase in sandbox](#test-coinbase-in-sandbox) covers Coinbase.

Sandbox has fewer providers and routes than production. Use it to prove your integration, then run your end-to-end checks in production.
