Headless is for teams who want the whole purchase to happen in their own app or site, payment included. White-Label API and Meld Checkout both hand the user to the onramp's UI to pay; headless keeps them in yours. Your backend creates an order, your client mounts the [Meld SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks), and the SDK draws the provider's card form or raises the Apple Pay or Google Pay sheet in place.

Headless also covers [transfers](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/transfers) from a customer's Coinbase account to their own wallet.

Meld keeps the parts you would rather not: routing between providers, KYC, compliance, and settlement tracking. Card details go straight to the provider's PCI surface, so they never reach your servers and your compliance scope does not widen.

---

## The order model

One endpoint creates both kinds of order:

**Endpoint:** [`POST /crypto/order/headless/onramp`](/api-reference/crypto/headless/crypto-order-headless-onramp-create)

`paymentMethodType` selects the method:

| `paymentMethodType` | What comes back | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CREDIT_DEBIT_CARD` | Provider card surface for the SDK to mount | [Cards](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/cards) |
| `APPLE_PAY` | Apple Pay surface for the SDK to present | [Wallets](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/wallets) |
| `GOOGLE_PAY` | Google Pay surface for the SDK to present | [Wallets](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/wallets#google-pay-with-coinbase) |

Transfers have their own endpoint, `POST /crypto/order/headless/transfer`, and their own order shape.

### The order

Every card and wallet order comes back in the same envelope. Only `paymentMethodResponseDetails` differs by method, and fields with nothing in them are omitted rather than returned null.

```json
{
  "id": "WoRdErabcexample0000001",
  "customerId": "WmYYgvN8ukpV62N3m4u3ee",
  "externalCustomerId": "your-internal-user-id-123",
  "externalOrderId": "order-8f21c0",
  "paymentMethodType": "CREDIT_DEBIT_CARD",
  "paymentMethodResponseDetails": { },
  "payload": { }
}
```

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | Meld's order id. Keep it — it is how you tie the order to the transaction later |
| `customerId` | Meld's id for the customer |
| `externalCustomerId` | Your own id for them, echoed back when you sent one |
| `externalOrderId` | Your own reference, echoed back |
| `paymentMethodType` | The method this order was created for |
| `paymentMethodResponseDetails` | The method-specific part of the order. Its shape varies by method and provider |
| `payload` | A verbatim echo of the request you sent, so you can correlate without re-threading your own state |

Pass the whole response to the [Meld SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks). Do not read individual fields to decide how to render: the SDK dispatches on the shape it is given, so adding a provider that uses an existing shape needs no change in your client.

### External order IDs and idempotency

`externalOrderId` is your own reference. It must be unique per account; reusing one is rejected. Generate a fresh id for every attempt.

Send `X-Idempotency-Key: <uuid>` on order creation. A repeated key returns the original order rather than creating a second one.

## Before you start

- A Meld API key with payment access (sandbox first, production for go-live)
- Headless onramps enabled on your account. Contact your Meld representative to enable Headless for your Meld account
- For production, your own account and credentials with each provider you use. See [provider credentials](#provider-credentials)
- A webhook endpoint configured in **Developer → Webhooks** in the dashboard

Cards and wallets are independent — integrate either on its own, or both. They share everything above and differ only in the surface the SDK presents, so adding the second later is a payment method and a capture surface, not a second integration.

Both need the [Meld SDK](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/sdks) in your client. Transfers need no SDK.

### Your customer must be KYC'd first

[Create the customer](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc/meld-kyces-the-user#step-1-create-a-meld-customer) and verify them through [Unified KYC](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc) before their first order. Almost every provider refuses an order for a customer Meld has not cleared, and the refusal arrives at order creation — after the customer has entered an amount and expects to pay.

Clearing the customer is not enough for providers that take their KYC as a share from Meld, such as Banxa. Name each of them in `kycShareProviders` when you start KYC, or add them later with a `PATCH` — see [Share KYC with providers](/docs/stablecoins/unified-kyc#share-kyc-with-providers). A provider you leave out refuses the order with `403 KYC_NOT_COMPLETED`.

Coinbase is the exception: it has a guest checkout, where the customer verifies their email and phone with a one-time code and accepts Coinbase's agreements, with no KYC. Once the customer reaches Coinbase's lifetime transaction limit, they must complete KYC to keep using Coinbase Apple Pay, through the `limitUpgrade` [process](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/shared-flows/eligibility#provider-processes). Stripe runs its own KYC, but complete Unified KYC first so the SDK can fill it. Transfers need no Unified KYC. See [Wallets → Providers](/docs/stablecoins/headless-integration/wallets#providers).

<Note>
  Some providers provision their own customer record at order time and reject the call without an email. If your users can sign up without one, sync it onto the Meld customer with `PATCH /accounts/customers/{customerId}` before the first order — a missing email surfaces later as a `404` naming a service-provider customer, which is harder to read than it sounds. Stripe and Coinbase check for it up front instead, and refuse the order with `403 VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` and `email: MISSING`.
</Note>

### Provider credentials

In production, every order runs on your own merchant account at the provider, using credentials registered to your Meld account. That includes Coinbase, with one exception today: Meld signs the one-time-code check of a Coinbase customer's email and phone with a Meld key, not yours.

For Coinbase, that is your own [Coinbase Developer Platform](https://portal.cdp.coinbase.com) (CDP) project:

- A Secret API key created with the **ECDSA** signature algorithm. Meld can't sign requests with an Ed25519 key, which is CDP's default for new keys
- The headless onramp's guest checkout enabled on the project, and Google Pay as well if you offer it. Coinbase turns these on by request: ask Coinbase or your Meld representative

Your Meld representative can register the key on your Meld account for you.

In sandbox, Meld enables Stripe, Coinbase and Banxa on your sandbox account with its own sandbox credentials. They appear under **Integrations** as **Shared Meld Integration**, and you add no keys or webhooks for them. Ask your Meld representative to enable them.
